# Backlog

Every open item across this repository's documents, condensed to one line each with a pointer to
where it is argued in full. Nothing here is new analysis — it is an index of decisions and work
that are already written down and not yet done, so a session can start from a list rather than a
re-read.

Status vocabulary: **defect** — the code is wrong; **decision** — nothing is wrong until someone
chooses; **work** — agreed, designed, unbuilt; **gap** — untested or unmeasured.

**An item's number is its name and never moves.** Other pages cite rows by id, so a row keeps its
number for life and a retired number is never reissued. Ordering carries the priority instead:
[What to do next](#what-to-do-next) ranks across the whole page, and within each section the rows
are ordered by what a player would notice first, which is why the numbers run out of sequence.

Every row here is open. What was fixed and why is recorded in the change log of the page that owns
it, which is also the page the **Where** column names — a finished item kept on two pages drifts on
one of them, and this one had drifted.

## What to do next

Ranked by what a player would feel, not by what is cheapest or most interesting. The first four are
the mod's own stated goals failing to reach the player; everything below them is the model being
wrong, or shipped at the wrong setting, somewhere a player can find.

| | Item | Why it is here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **C11** — damage arrives faster than a player can react to it | The mod's own `G5` says a player must be able to react to a warning. The median ship crosses critical **8.9 s** after full load, p10 at 3.5 s. The corpus asks for this decision more clearly than for any other. |
| 2 | **B25** — no feedback reaches a player who is not looking at an instrument | Heat is only a resource a player reasons about if they learn about it. No sound, particle or emissive code exists anywhere in the mod. |
| 3 | **C2** — `Cubes.xml` has not been retuned since the model changed under it | Every block's authored numbers predate three changes to the physics that reads them, so the balance a player meets is not the balance anyone chose. |
| 4 | **C10** — no vanilla prefab has ever been simulated | The stated compatibility floor is that a ship the game spawns must not fall apart on arrival, and nothing measures it. 705 files already in the install, one parser element away. |
| 5 | **A17** — the shipped defaults diverge on a burning ship | A shipped configuration reaching 10,000 K and never settling is the most visible way the model can be wrong, and the published cause was falsified. |
| 6 | **A9** — occlusion ships its cheapest rung as the default | A ship crossing a terminator flips between fully lit and fully dark at the moment its centre passes. The ladder above that is mostly built and switched off, and *fidelity is the default* says the cheap form is the option, not the shipped behaviour. |
| 7 | **C12** — the significance window is unreachable at the shipped clock | The balance target asks the most significant thermal event to land in 2–5 minutes. No block in the game lands in it, and none can be made to at `HeatTimeScale` 225. |
| 8 | **B4** — client temperatures drift from the server's and are never corrected | Cosmetic today, because damage is server-only; it is the readout a player trusts. |
| 9 | **D19** — nothing is threaded | A 242-grid fleet spends 25.9 % of real time in the solver on the game's own thread, which is the number that decides whether a server can run this. |

## A. Correctness defects

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A17 | **The shipped defaults diverge on a burning 4,000-block ship with 0% of their substeps refused.** `x-burning-ship` passes 10,000 K and never settles. Starvation was the published explanation and is falsified: `candidate`, refused 49% on the same rig, lands one kelvin away. The cause is unknown, so the next step is to find it rather than to fix it — `MaxSubsteps` is already granted in full. Pinned by `ProfileSuiteTests.TheShippedProfileStillDivergesOnABurningShip`. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md#open-defects) |
| A16 | **A buried grid convects with the planet's air coefficient.** Nothing under the surface is against air, but rock contact is not modelled, so the environment exchange stays at `PlanetConvectionCoefficient` — 50 W/(m²·K) at 20 km down. Ambient is right there (the rock's own temperature) so the direction of the exchange is right and the rate is a guess. What it should be is a conduction against rock, which needs a contact area the model does not compute. Visible on `-- descent`. | [environment.md](environment.md#underground) |
| A9 | **Occlusion should be a ladder of configurations, and the most realistic one should be the default.** Today it is three interacting switches with no name for the thing they compose, and the cheapest rung ships. **Built already**, and the rungs of the ladder: `none`, no occluder; **`grid`**, one ray from the centre, the whole ship lit or dark together and flipping the moment its centre crosses — the simplest solution, and the shipped default at `SolarOcclusionSamples 1`; **`sampled`**, up to nine points inset in the hull with gain scaled by the share that reached the sun, so a terminator crossing ramps; **`faces`**, where the grid's own hull (`SolarSelfShadowing`) and other grids (`SolarGridShadows = full`) already land their shadows on the faces they actually cover. **Unbuilt, and the top rung: planet, terrain and voxel shadow resolved per face**, so a capital ship at a terminator carries a real shadow edge across its own hull instead of dimming uniformly. **The three occluders are not one job.** A planet is `OcclusionMath.IsOccludedBySphere` — a normalise, a dot and an `atan`, no ray at all — so evaluating it per exposed face is arithmetic on a pass that already exists, and it is the rung that matters because day and night is the dominant solar event; note that `OcclusionThreshold` is a fitted curve by its own admission, and an edge crossing a hull makes that fit visible where a whole-grid flag hid it. Terrain and voxels are the expensive two, and the standing objection — that per-block scales the ray count with block count — is true only of them; the route worth trying is the one `SolarGridShadows = full` already uses for neighbour grids, resolving each occluder to a silhouette once per interval and testing faces against it rather than casting per face. **Why the default moves**: shipping `grid` contradicts *fidelity is the default; a saving is a switch*, and a ship flipping between fully lit and fully dark is exactly the perceptible difference that clause says must not be the cheap form. Costing the top rung needs `F5` first — terrain occlusion has never been measured in game. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md#deliberate-limits), [configuration.md](configuration.md#external-shadow), [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#fidelity-is-the-default-a-saving-is-a-switch) |
| A4 | **`SweepRoomPressure` is the one whole-grid pass that is not a rota or a budgeted slice.** Two game API calls per compartment every eight steps, bounded by compartment count rather than block count. Measured at 0.054 % of real time on twelve compartments, so the rota is deliberately deferred: it is a station-scale risk with no evidence behind it, and the sweep is now timed with counters so the next dump from a large station answers with a number. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md#open-defects) |

## B. Player-facing gaps and decisions

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B25 | **No feedback reaches a player who is not looking at an instrument.** No sound emitter, no particle effect and no emissive code exists anywhere in the mod. Intent is subtle and in-world rather than an alarm: metal ticking and pinging as it expands, joints under a gradient, air distorting around a hot block in atmosphere, and a block glowing as it climbs. The trigger is a ratio the solver already computes every step. **Shape decided**: audio is cockpit-only, a lead cue about three seconds out and a distinct one at the crossing; visuals are a linear ramp keyed to each block's threshold temperature, full at critical and gone at ordinary temperatures. **Still open**: the cue itself; that a linear projection from `LastDeltaTemperature` over-warns on a block that is levelling off; and where the ramp starts, since radiated power rises as the fourth power and a band defined as a fraction of critical is not a band defined as a number of seconds. It is also the first presentation feature meant to be on by default, so its cost when nothing is hot must be a threshold test and nothing more. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#natural-feedback--not-built) |
| B4 | **Block temperatures are not reconciled between server and clients.** Settings and the two pump throttles replicate, and `/thermal sync` compares a client against the server by digest; temperatures do not. Clients re-simulate from the same inputs and drift, and a client joining mid-session starts from saved values. Damage is server-only, so the divergence is cosmetic — but how far a client may drift before it is corrected is undecided. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md#open-defects) |
| B24 | **A thermal camera view, and no known way to build one.** Mods get no shader, no post-process and no frame buffer; blanking the world and redrawing every body as a billboard gives coarse terrain, discs for asteroids and nothing for what the mod does not draw, which is why the first heat overlay was removed. **Wanted, and the route is expected to be indirect** — whether any shader or material parameter is reachable at all, transparent materials as the extinguisher overlay already uses, particle effects as a rendering surface, and per-block emissive, which is the one path the engine definitely exposes. **Research before design.** | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#thermal-vision--wanted-method-unknown) |
| B8 | **Three things are left on the settings surface**: Status as the mod's own report rather than a list of what changed — cost per stage, load as a projection, and the faults that have each been a real defect at least once — the config file regrouped by system with a migration off the flat shape, and per-subtype block overrides for the 432 values in `Cubes.xml`, written into world storage because a workshop mod folder is read-only. | B17 | **A parked grid can be pushed over the friction threshold by wind alone.** 4.2% of field samples exceeded `FrictionAtSpeedsAbove` at 50 m/s; the offline storm scenario reaches **209 m/s with 19% of samples over the threshold and 49% over the engine's own ceiling**. The fix is a balance call — cap the composed speed at the ceiling, lower the storm fraction, or accept that a storm at 150 m is genuinely violent. | [environment.md](environment.md#measuring-it) |
| B14 | **The wind's meridional component is poleward everywhere and reverses across the equator.** Air diverges from the equator — Earth's trades converge — and the vector flips end for end at latitude 0. `TheWindSwingsThroughTheCalmsRatherThanReversingAcrossALine` exists to prevent exactly this and measures only the east component, so it passes. Nothing tests the north-south half of `WindField.Direction` at all. **The largest known fault in the pattern.** | [environment.md](environment.md#limits-and-open-questions) |
| B18 | **The engine's figure is no longer a ceiling.** 3 field samples exceeded it (80.3 m/s against 72.9) once profile and band share compound. The whole justification for using `GetWindSpeed` was that it bounds the wind; it now only scales it. Decide whether to reinstate the bound. | [environment.md](environment.md#wind-the-whole-of-it) |
| B19 | **Every terrain factor saturates its cap on ordinary SE ground.** SE hills reach 12% of a planet's radius against Earth's 0.14%, so terrain is ~80× steeper than the wind-engineering literature the caps came from. Speed-up, shelter and channelling all run into their limits on every shipped world at every size — the caps are doing the modelling rather than bounding it. Worth re-deriving them for SE slopes rather than terrestrial ones. | [environment.md](environment.md#world-size-and-why-every-borrowed-constant-lands-oddly) |
| B20 | **The shipped boundary layer is taller than several shipped atmospheres.** `WindGradientHeight` is 600 m; Titan, Europa and the Moon have atmospheres 285–570 m deep, and Triton's peaks stand above its air entirely. The profile is being evaluated in vacuum and only the ceiling reaching zero keeps it sane. A per-planet gradient height derived from `AtmosphereAltitude` would be the honest fix. | [environment.md](environment.md#world-size-and-why-every-borrowed-constant-lands-oddly) |
| B16 | **Roughness length is one number for a whole world.** The ground material under a grid is already classified for the temperature model, and roughness is exactly what it should vary with — snow smooth, forest rough. `WindRoughnessLength` is a world setting instead. | [environment.md](environment.md#what-the-solver-computes) |
| B22 | **Seven of the eight shipped worlds have air density exactly 1**, so swing, pole drop, lag and convection derive to the same figure for all of them — only the airless Moon differs. Gravity (lapse rate) and solar protection are the only real differentiation between atmospheric worlds. Whether to author density per world in this mod's own file, against the engine's, is open. | [environment.md](environment.md#limits-and-open-questions) |
| B28 | **Three wind mechanisms are named as not built**: sea breeze, gap-wind acceleration and lee turbulence. Recorded so the absence is a decision rather than an oversight; none is required by anything else on this page. | [environment.md](environment.md#limits-and-open-questions) |
| B23 | **`GetTemperatureInPoint` returns zero wherever there is no oxygen**, so the `game_temperature` telemetry column is flatly zero on Mars, Europa and the Moon. It is a 0..1 comfort figure rather than kelvin in any case. **Measured**: over 3,465 breathable rows it correlates +0.86 with the sun's elevation and +0.12 with this model's ambient, so it is a daylight figure and not a cross-check on anything thermal. The telemetry should say so where it is reported. | [environment.md](environment.md#temperature-two-things-and-neither-is-a-temperature) |
| B26 | **The readouts should be indistinguishable from the game's own HUD.** They are drawn through Rich HUD so they can adopt its placement, scaling and styling; the goal is that a player reads a temperature the way they read a power figure, with nothing mod-shaped announcing itself. No check covers this and none obviously can — it is a judgement made by looking. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#instruments--built) |
| B9 | **A startup crash in `ThermalGrid.Tick`, seen once and not reproducible.** The tick is guarded and logs its inner frames, and faults record whatever the collection state, so a recurrence names itself rather than taking the session down or being discarded with telemetry off. Watching, not chasing. | [telemetry.md](telemetry.md#faults-are-recorded-whether-or-not-collection-is-running) |

## C. Balance and tuning

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C11 | **Damage arrives faster than a player can react to it.** Of ships that cross critical under full electrical load the median crosses at **8.9 s** and p10 at 3.5 s. `G5` as literally written — bounded recovery — holds; the rationale given for it, *"a player must be able to react to a warning"*, does not. This is not a defect and it is the balance decision the corpus most clearly asks for, and it is upstream of B25: a warning cue has nothing to warn with if the whole event is nine seconds long. | [balance.md](balance.md#damage-arrives-too-fast-to-be-played-around), [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#where-the-goals-and-the-code-disagree) |
| C2 | **`Cubes.xml` has not been retuned since the model changed under it.** Radiators conduct at half their old rate, fast flight heats the leading face where it used to compute friction and discard it, and overheat damage is per simulated second rather than per step. The authored values predate all three. | [balance.md](balance.md#block-balance) |
| C10 | **No vanilla prefab has ever been simulated.** Stated intent is that a ship the game spawns — cargo ships, drones, encounters, respawn ships — must not fall apart on arrival, and nothing measures it. There are **705** prefab files under `Content/Data/Prefabs` (46 planetary encounters, 41 unknown signals, 38 global encounters, 32 random encounters, 23 cargo ships, 47 drones, 2 respawn). | C12 | **The 2–5 minute significance window is unreachable at the shipped clock.** The balance target asks for the most significant thermal event to land in 2–5 minutes; of the 72 block types that cannot cool themselves, **none** falls in 120–300 s, and none can be made to at `HeatTimeScale` 225. The dial that reaches it alone is `HeatTimeScale` ≈ 11, which pushes a hull's settling time to eight hours. Raising conduction and lowering the clock **together** is the projected route and has never been measured — every knob sweep run so far moves one dial. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#where-the-goals-and-the-code-disagree), [balance.md](balance.md) |
| C13 | **A coolant loop draws no power.** `CoolantPump.MaxPowerWatts` is declared and never assigned anywhere, so a loop is free to run once built, where a heat pump pays for every watt it lifts. The asymmetry may well be right — a circulator is not a refrigerator — but nobody has stated it, and the stated intent is that a cooling system costs power and makes heat doing it. A decision, not a fix. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#a-cooling-system-costs-power-and-makes-heat-doing-it) |
| C3 | **Whether to ship `MaxSubstepsPerBlock 6` / `MaxSubsteps 6`** against the current `0` / `64` at `Frequency 8`. In the field it takes floored blocks from 9.0 % to 1.03 % and drift from 2.89 K to 1.07 K, for 1.63 % → 2.17 % of real time. At the shipped eighth-second step a cap of 6 buys 1.6x for 459 blocks of 43,232 and 0.11 K worst error; driven over 500 simulated seconds the peak is unmoved down to cap 3. A clear win, and not a dramatic one. | [stiffness.md](stiffness.md#1-a-per-block-substep-cap--the-one-that-is-built) |
| C4 | **The harness catalog disagrees with the shipped definitions, and every scenario temperature is quoted off it.** `Catalog` masses are up to 4× out — Battery 1,040 kg against 3,845; Thruster 10,000 against 43,200; Radiator 900 against 600 — and `Catalog.ReactorThermal` carries `ProducerWasteEnergy` 0.25 against the shipped 0.01, so any scenario quoting a reactor temperature quotes one no player will see. `Vanilla.Reference` derives from real build costs and is the model `Catalog` should follow. | [balance.md](balance.md#open-items-in-the-block-report) |
| C14 | **The census hull is quoted both as "a worst case" and as "what a ship does".** It makes 12.1 kW a block against a real median of 335 W — the 96th percentile of the corpus. Undecided, and recorded as such in the code: `TheCensusHullMakesFarMoreHeatThanARealShip` pins the figure and fails if it moves quietly. It reaches every temperature figure in the repository and no stiffness figure. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#where-the-goals-and-the-code-disagree) |
| C15 | **No shipped block declares a selective surface, now that one can.** `SolarAbsorptivity` is separate from `Emissivity`, and every block still absorbs at its emissivity because that is the default. The obvious candidate is the mod's own radiator, which is authored at emissivity 0.35 and therefore absorbs a third of the sun that lands on it — a real radiator is nearer `α 0.1 / ε 0.8`. Whether to author it is a balance question with a measurement behind it: it makes a sunlit radiator better and changes nothing in shadow, and the sunlit corpus scenario is where the size of that would show. | [definitions.md](definitions.md#emissivity-and-absorptivity-are-two-numbers) |
| C16 | **The suit's cooler is free.** `SuitThermal` computes what the regulation moves and charges nothing for it, because `IMyCharacter` exposes `SuitEnergyLevel` to read and nothing to write — a mod cannot take a player's suit charge. That is an engine limit rather than a decision, and it sits beside `C13`: both are places the mod's own *a cooling system costs power* does not reach. What the mod can see is a flat suit, and a flat suit does not regulate, so the coupling exists in the one direction the game allows. Revisit if a setter ever appears. |
| C17 | **Only room air can hurt a player.** The suit reads the air of a pressurised compartment this mod has mapped, and nothing else: a player on Mercury's day side, in a vacuum in full sun, or inside an open-frame ship with a reactor beside them is unsimulated. Room air was where the temperature already was; ambient outside a grid needs the environment solved at a point rather than for a grid, which `PlanetManager` could answer. The scope is stated in [configuration.md](configuration.md#the-suit) so it reads as a boundary rather than as safety. |
| C18 | **Cold does not hurt.** The suit regulates both ways and reports the watts it puts back in, and a player whose suit is flat in shadow cools without ever being damaged — there is a `SuitCriticalTemperature` and no floor beside it. Hypothermia starts around 308 K, which is four degrees under where the suit holds them, so the number is not hard to choose; what is undecided is whether freezing to death should be a thing this mod does at all, given a suit that has no charge is already a problem the game has its own answer to. |
| C5 | The underground core gradient sits behind a 2 km sea-level deadzone, deeper than SE's voxels reach, so every reachable depth reads a flat `UndergroundTemperature`. Whether the default should be a few hundred metres is a balance question. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md), [environment.md](environment.md#limits-and-open-questions) |
| C6 | `AmbientLagSeconds` is 45 absolute seconds against a day that is not: it attenuates a four-minute day to 46 % and does nothing to a two-hour one. Wants expressing as a share of `MySectorWeatherComponent.RotationInterval`, if that type is reachable under the whitelist. | [environment.md](environment.md#limits-and-open-questions) |
| C7 | The 4 K/km lapse rate and the ground and convection offset tables are opinions chosen to look like Earth, not fitted. The honest fix is probably that ground offsets shrink as the lapse rate grows, since a site is only snowy *because* it is high. The snow reference site is where that will show. | [environment.md](environment.md#limits-and-open-questions) |
| C8 | Scenario run lengths are fixed to the shipped clock, so 59 of 136 cells of the realism comparison are still climbing when a run ends. Run lengths would have to scale with `HeatTimeScale`. | [realism.md](realism.md#known-limits-of-this-suite) |

## D. Performance and scale

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D19 | **Nothing is threaded, and the intent is that as much as possible should be.** Use as much of the CPU as possible while taking as little of the game thread as possible; the two halves are one goal, since the game's simulation stability is set by what runs on its own thread. The solver is already safe to split — order independence is an invariant, not an accident — and `MyAPIGateway.Parallel` provides `For`, `ForEach`, `Do` and `Start`. The natural shape is *solve in parallel, apply on the game thread* via `InvokeOnGameThread`, because reads must not race the game mutating a grid. **Measure before adopting, and note the two figures point opposite ways**: 8,000 blocks solve in 0.128 ms a step, possibly under the cost of a hand-off, while a 242-grid fleet spent 25.9 % of real time in the solver. Per-grid parallelism across many grids is a different change from splitting one huge grid, and the fleet figure argues for it first. How many threads, and whether the mod may saturate a machine it shares with other mods, is undecided. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#use-the-machine-and-stay-off-the-game-thread) |
| D1 | A solver step is atomic and 118 ms at a million blocks, down from 623 ms. A step must touch every node, so only activity tracking, chunking and multirate make it smaller; all designed, none built. Whether what is left is near the memory-bandwidth floor is now an open question rather than an answer — the last time that was claimed, two passes were reaching through the node objects instead. | [scale-design.md](scale-design.md), [load-and-hitching.md](load-and-hitching.md#what-is-still-open) |
| D2 | The room map floods the bounding volume — 14× the block count on a hull — and takes 7,207 ticks (twenty minutes) to converge at a million blocks. Wants the host gas system, with a coarse flood as fallback. | [scale-design.md](scale-design.md) §4 |
| D15 | **A frame spikes to 68 ms every thirty seconds and nothing says why.** Five frames of the 2026-08-20 fleet dump, evenly spaced, each reporting 2.0-2.8 ms of solver and zero topology, rooms and exposure against a 67-72 ms total. Nothing in the mod runs on that period — the mass sweep is 8 steps and the hottest-node scan 4. The environment sample and the after-step observation are now timed and the cost table carries an `unattributed` row, so the next dump attributes it or narrows it to pacing. | [telemetry.md](telemetry.md#what-the-stages-leave-over) |
| D4 | The first step of a grid's life spikes on first touch of every flat array: 28.3 ms above a 13.2 ms tick at half a million blocks, and 53 ms above a 26 ms tick at a million. A warm-up rather than a stutter, and still the largest number in the distribution at the top rung. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md) |
| D3 | World load is 11 s at a million blocks in one call. Acceptable behind a loading screen; a blueprint pasted mid-session takes the same path. | [load-and-hitching.md](load-and-hitching.md#what-is-still-open) |
| D14 | **Spreading a step costs a large grid 5-13 % in cache.** Between two of its frames the other grids in the world evict its arrays, so every resume is a cold start. Staggering whole steps across frames instead would keep each grid's data hot and cost one dispatch, at the price of a lump per grid — the trade the current design was chosen to avoid, and worth re-deciding now the lump is measurable. | [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md#below-eight-hundred-blocks) |
| D13 | **A grid is visited every frame whether or not the frame does solver work.** `bench smallgrids` now measures that visit on its own — 5.9 ns a grid a frame, flat with grid size, 24 % of what a one-block grid costs and 70 % of its pacing overhead. No change to a step can reach it; only visiting fewer grids per frame can. | [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md#below-eight-hundred-blocks) |
| D5 | Lumping (merge a stiff node into its dominant neighbour at topology time) — exact where the substep cap is approximate, and removes links as well as stiffness. Worth a few per cent on SE1 fleets; the design work is unlumping incrementally on change. | [stiffness.md](stiffness.md#2-lumping--the-same-idea-done-properly) |
| D6 | Multirate stepping, and implicit integration as the endgame. Both currently lose to the per-block cap on an SE1 fleet; both are the answer when the stiff set is not a thin tail, i.e. SE2. | [stiffness.md](stiffness.md#3-multirate-stepping--when-the-tail-is-not-thin) |
| D18 | **The relaxation row is all ones for ninety-five per cent of nodes and is written for all of them.** Skipping the divide for the unbound ones was tried, is exact, and measured as nothing against a 4-6 % run-to-run spread — so the row's ~1 ns a node is the two loads and the store, not the arithmetic. A future attempt should aim at not writing the row: the conduction loop reads it per link end regardless, so this needs a sparse representation or a per-grid flag rather than a cheaper sum. Only live when a grid is refused the substeps it asked for. | [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md#the-relaxation-rows-cost-is-the-row-not-the-divide--tried-and-dropped) |

## E. Memory

Local changes, no design work behind them, ~20 % of a grid's footprint between them. Ordered by
saving, since none of them is visible to a player.

| # | Item | Saving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E3 | **Room cells are held twice**, once per room and once in `roomIndexByCell`. One `Vector3I[]` sorted by room with an `int[]` of range starts holds both at 12 bytes a cell — but the dictionary answers `RegionOf` on the exposure path, so this means giving that lookup a different shape rather than deleting it. The only row that still climbs with grid size. | ~9 MB at 126k, 47 at 500k |
