# Backlog

Every open item across this repository's documents, condensed to one line each with a pointer to
where it is argued in full. Nothing here is new analysis — it is an index of decisions and work
that are already written down and not yet done, so a session can start from a list rather than a
re-read.

Status vocabulary: **defect** — the code is wrong; **decision** — nothing is wrong until someone
chooses; **work** — agreed, designed, unbuilt; **gap** — untested or unmeasured.

**An item's number is its name and never moves.** Other pages cite rows by id, so a row keeps its
number for life and a retired number is never reissued. Ordering carries the priority instead:
[What to do next](#what-to-do-next) ranks across the whole page, and within each section the rows
are ordered by what a player would notice first, which is why the numbers run out of sequence.

Every row here is open. What was fixed and why is recorded in the change log of the page that owns
it, which is also the page the **Where** column names — a finished item kept on two pages drifts on
one of them, and this one had drifted.

## D. Performance and scale

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D19 | **Nothing is threaded, the intent is that as much as possible should be, and the fleet half is now measured.** Use as much of the CPU as possible while taking as little of the game thread as possible. The solver is already safe to split — order independence is an invariant — and `MyAPIGateway.Parallel` provides `For`, `ForEach`, `Do` and `Start`; the shape is *solve in parallel, apply on the game thread* through `InvokeOnGameThread`. **Measured 2026-08-23 and the doubt is resolved**: fanning out over 242 work items costs **1.6–6.8 µs** against a 1,004-node grid's own 0.54 ms, so a single grid through the fan-out is 0.99× and a 242-grid fleet is **10.17×** on 32 threads, **7.09×** on eight — which takes the field's 25.9 % of real time to about 2.5 % and 3.7 %. `FleetParallelTests` pins that a fleet stepped one grid per thread is bit-identical to one stepped in order, and that every static field in `Core` is named with the reason two grids may share it. **What is left is the build, and three things the rig cannot answer**: the engine's own scheduler rather than the framework's, the game-thread half of the work, and how many threads a mod may take on a machine it shares. **And the bound is the largest ship** — an uneven fleet gives 3.35×, because a step cannot finish before its biggest grid does, which is the argument for splitting one grid as a second change rather than a substitute. | [scale-design.md](scale-design.md#one-grid-per-thread-measured), [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#use-the-machine-and-stay-off-the-game-thread) |

| D14 | **Spreading a step costs a fleet up to 12 %, and what staggering gives up is not what the row assumed.** Measured on the fleet rather than on one grid (`bench stagger`, identical arithmetic either way so the difference is locality): **12.2 %** on 242 grids of 1,004 nodes, 5.5 % on 64 of 2,223, and **nothing at all on four** — the penalty is the fleet's working set rather than any grid's size, which is why the single-grid ladder could not see it. **The lump is 0.53 ms** for a 1,004-node grid and flat in fleet size, and a frame carries the same total work under either schedule — `grids × Frequency / 60` steps' worth — so staggering does not add work to a frame, it makes the work belong to fewer grids. ## F. Testing and measurement gaps

Ordered by how much a published figure depends on the gap.

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F17 | **Thrust is not in the degraded-input sweep, and it is the input most likely to be worst.** `ThermalBlock` feeds the solver `ForceMagnitude × CurrentThrust / MaxThrust` as a heat term separate from electrical power, and `CurrentThrust` is physics state — which the engine *predicts* on a client rather than replicating, so a client's thrust is its own guess about a ship it is not simulating the physics of. On a burning ship that is the largest heat term there is, and `-- inputs` degrades electrical power only. The work is a thrust knob in `ClientInputLab` and a scenario that flies rather than sits, since a hull at rest makes the knob measure nothing. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md#the-nine-cases-are-not-the-whole-input-surface-and-here-is-what-is-missing) |
| F18 | **Solar occlusion is the one client input that is a binary, and it is unmodelled.** Every other degraded input is wrong by an amount; `IsSolarOccluded` is wrong by the entire solar term at once, and it is resolved by raycasting against voxels and neighbouring grids — world state a client holds differently and on its own budget. A ship a client believes is in shade while the server has it in full sun is the largest single-step input error available.
