# Backlog

Every open item across this repository's documents, condensed to one line each with a pointer to
where it is argued in full. Nothing here is new analysis — it is an index of decisions and work
that are already written down and not yet done, so a session can start from a list rather than a
re-read.

Status vocabulary: **defect** — the code is wrong; **decision** — nothing is wrong until someone
chooses; **work** — agreed, designed, unbuilt; **gap** — untested or unmeasured.

**An item's number is its name and never moves.** Other pages cite rows by id, so a row keeps its
number for life and a retired number is never reissued. Ordering carries the priority instead:
[What to do next](#what-to-do-next) ranks across the whole page, and within each section the rows
are ordered by what a player would notice first, which is why the numbers run out of sequence.

Every row here is open. What was fixed and why is recorded in the change log of the page that owns
it, which is also the page the **Where** column names — a finished item kept on two pages drifts on
one of them, and this one had drifted.

## What to do next

Ranked by what a player would feel, not by what is cheapest or most interesting. The first three are
the mod's own stated goals failing to reach the player; everything below them is the model being
wrong, or shipped at the wrong setting, somewhere a player can find.

| | Item | Why it is here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **C11** — damage arrives faster than a player can react to it | The mod's own `G5` says a player must be able to react to a warning. The median ship crosses critical **8.9 s** after full load, p10 at 3.5 s. The corpus asks for this decision more clearly than for any other. |
| 2 | **C2** — `Cubes.xml` has not been retuned since the model changed under it | Every block's authored numbers predate three changes to the physics that reads them, so the balance a player meets is not the balance anyone chose. |
| 3 | **A9** — occlusion ships its cheapest rung as the default | A ship crossing a terminator flips between fully lit and fully dark at the moment its centre passes. The ladder above that is mostly built and switched off, and *fidelity is the default* says the cheap form is the option, not the shipped behaviour. |
| 4 | **C12** — the significance window is unreachable at the shipped clock | The balance target asks the most significant thermal event to land in 2–5 minutes. No block in the game lands in it, and none can be made to at `HeatTimeScale` 225. |
| 5 | **B4** — a client's readout is wrong about critical for longer than the event lasts | Measured: a joining client misreads which side of critical a block is on for 150–305 s, against a damage event that is a median 8.9 s long. It converges on its own, and not before the thing it was reporting is over. |
| 6 | **D19** — nothing is threaded | A 242-grid fleet spends 25.9 % of real time in the solver on the game's own thread, which is the number that decides whether a server can run this. |

## A. Correctness defects

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A9 | **Occlusion should be a ladder of configurations, and the most realistic one should be the default.** Today it is three interacting switches with no name for the thing they compose, and the cheapest rung ships. **Built already**, and the rungs of the ladder: `none`, no occluder; **`grid`**, one ray from the centre, the whole ship lit or dark together and flipping the moment its centre crosses — the simplest solution, and the shipped default at `SolarOcclusionSamples 1`; **`sampled`**, up to nine points inset in the hull with gain scaled by the share that reached the sun, so a terminator crossing ramps; **`faces`**, where the grid's own hull (`SolarSelfShadowing`) and other grids (`SolarGridShadows = full`) already land their shadows on the faces they actually cover. **Unbuilt, and the top rung: planet, terrain and voxel shadow resolved per face**, so a capital ship at a terminator carries a real shadow edge across its own hull instead of dimming uniformly. **The three occluders are not one job.** A planet is `OcclusionMath.IsOccludedBySphere` — a normalise, a dot and an `atan`, no ray at all — so evaluating it per exposed face is arithmetic on a pass that already exists, and it is the rung that matters because day and night is the dominant solar event; note that `OcclusionThreshold` is a fitted curve by its own admission, and an edge crossing a hull makes that fit visible where a whole-grid flag hid it. Terrain and voxels are the expensive two, and the standing objection — that per-block scales the ray count with block count — is true only of them; the route worth trying is the one `SolarGridShadows = full` already uses for neighbour grids, resolving each occluder to a silhouette once per interval and testing faces against it rather than casting per face. **Why the default moves**: shipping `grid` contradicts *fidelity is the default; a saving is a switch*, and a ship flipping between fully lit and fully dark is exactly the perceptible difference that clause says must not be the cheap form. Costing the top rung needs `F5` first — terrain occlusion has never been measured in game. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md#deliberate-limits), [configuration.md](configuration.md#external-shadow), [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#fidelity-is-the-default-a-saving-is-a-switch) |
| A4 | **`SweepRoomPressure` is the one whole-grid pass that is not a rota or a budgeted slice.** Two game API calls per compartment every eight steps, bounded by compartment count rather than block count. Measured at 0.054 % of real time on twelve compartments, so the rota is deliberately deferred: it is a station-scale risk with no evidence behind it, and the sweep is now timed with counters so the next dump from a large station answers with a number. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md#open-defects) |

## C. Balance and tuning

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C11 | **Damage arrives faster than a player can react to it.** Of ships that cross critical under full electrical load the median crosses at **8.9 s** and p10 at 3.5 s. `G5` as literally written — bounded recovery — holds; the rationale given for it, *"a player must be able to react to a warning"*, does not. This is not a defect and it is the balance decision the corpus most clearly asks for, and it is upstream of B25: a warning cue has nothing to warn with if the whole event is nine seconds long. | [balance.md](balance.md#damage-arrives-too-fast-to-be-played-around), [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#where-the-goals-and-the-code-disagree) |
| C2 | **`Cubes.xml` has not been retuned since the model changed under it.** Radiators conduct at half their old rate, fast flight heats the leading face where it used to compute friction and discard it, and overheat damage is per simulated second rather than per step. The authored values predate all three. | [balance.md](balance.md#block-balance) |
| C12 | **The 2–5 minute significance window is unreachable at the shipped clock.** The balance target asks for the most significant thermal event to land in 2–5 minutes; of the 72 block types that cannot cool themselves, **none** falls in 120–300 s, and none can be made to at `HeatTimeScale` 225. The dial that reaches it alone is `HeatTimeScale` ≈ 11, which pushes a hull's settling time to eight hours. Raising conduction and lowering the clock **together** is the projected route and has never been measured — every knob sweep run so far moves one dial. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#where-the-goals-and-the-code-disagree), [balance.md](balance.md) |
| C3 | **Whether to ship `MaxSubstepsPerBlock 6` / `MaxSubsteps 6`** against the current `0` / `64` at `Frequency 8`. In the field it takes floored blocks from 9.0 % to 1.03 % and drift from 2.89 K to 1.07 K, for 1.63 % → 2.17 % of real time. At the shipped eighth-second step a cap of 6 buys 1.6x for 459 blocks of 43,232 and 0.11 K worst error; driven over 500 simulated seconds the peak is unmoved down to cap 3. A clear win, and not a dramatic one. | [stiffness.md](stiffness.md#1-a-per-block-substep-cap--the-one-that-is-built) |
| C4 | **The harness catalog disagrees with the shipped definitions, and every scenario temperature is quoted off it.** **Measured and pinned** by `CatalogDriftTests`: four of the six blocks `Catalog` stands in for are out by more than five per cent, and the worst is **4.32×** — the large thruster at 10,000 kg against a real 43,200 — with the battery at 3.70× because it carries the *small-grid* battery's 1,040 kg under a large-grid name. Armour matches exactly, which is what makes the other four a measurement rather than an artefact of the comparison. And `Catalog.ReactorThermal` carries `ProducerWasteEnergy` **0.25 against the shipped 0.01**, so any scenario quoting a reactor temperature quotes one twenty-five times over-driven. `Vanilla.Reference` derives from real build costs and is the model `Catalog` should follow. **The fix moves every scenario figure in the repository at once, so it is a pass of its own**; the drift is pinned meanwhile so it cannot grow quietly. | [balance.md](balance.md#open-items-in-the-block-report) |
| C14 | **The census hull is quoted both as "a worst case" and as "what a ship does".** It makes 12.1 kW a block against a real median of 335 W — the 96th percentile of the corpus. Undecided, and recorded as such in the code: `TheCensusHullMakesFarMoreHeatThanARealShip` pins the figure and fails if it moves quietly. It reaches every temperature figure in the repository and no stiffness figure. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#where-the-goals-and-the-code-disagree) |
| C15 | **No shipped block declares a selective surface, now that one can.** `SolarAbsorptivity` is separate from `Emissivity`, and every block still absorbs at its emissivity because that is the default. The obvious candidate is the mod's own radiator, which is authored at emissivity 0.35 and therefore absorbs a third of the sun that lands on it — a real radiator is nearer `α 0.1 / ε 0.8`. Whether to author it is a balance question with a measurement behind it: it makes a sunlit radiator better and changes nothing in shadow, and the sunlit corpus scenario is where the size of that would show. | [definitions.md](definitions.md#emissivity-and-absorptivity-are-two-numbers) |
| C16 | **The suit's cooler is free.** `SuitThermal` computes what the regulation moves and charges nothing for it, because `IMyCharacter` exposes `SuitEnergyLevel` to read and nothing to write — a mod cannot take a player's suit charge. That is an engine limit rather than a decision, and it sits beside `C13`: both are places the mod's own *a cooling system costs power* does not reach. What the mod can see is a flat suit, and a flat suit does not regulate, so the coupling exists in the one direction the game allows. Revisit if a setter ever appears. |
| C17 | **Only room air can hurt a player.** The suit reads the air of a pressurised compartment this mod has mapped, and nothing else: a player on Mercury's day side, in a vacuum in full sun, or inside an open-frame ship with a reactor beside them is unsimulated. Room air was where the temperature already was; ambient outside a grid needs the environment solved at a point rather than for a grid, which `PlanetManager` could answer. The scope is stated in [configuration.md](configuration.md#the-suit) so it reads as a boundary rather than as safety. |
| C18 | **Cold does not hurt.** The suit regulates both ways and reports the watts it puts back in, and a player whose suit is flat in shadow cools without ever being damaged — there is a `SuitCriticalTemperature` and no floor beside it. Hypothermia starts around 308 K, which is four degrees under where the suit holds them, so the number is not hard to choose; what is undecided is whether freezing to death should be a thing this mod does at all, given a suit that has no charge is already a problem the game has its own answer to. |
| C7 | The 4 K/km lapse rate and the ground and convection offset tables are opinions chosen to look like Earth, not fitted. The honest fix is probably that ground offsets shrink as the lapse rate grows, since a site is only snowy *because* it is high. The snow reference site is where that will show. | [environment.md](environment.md#limits-and-open-questions) |
| C8 | Scenario run lengths are fixed to the shipped clock, so 72 of 136 cells of the realism comparison are still climbing when a run ends. Run lengths would have to scale with `HeatTimeScale`. It is also the reason the one non-starved divergence left in the sweep — `physical / x-overloaded` — cannot be told apart from a run that was stopped too early. | [realism.md](realism.md#known-limits-of-this-suite) |

## D. Performance and scale

| # | Item | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D19 | **Nothing is threaded, and the intent is that as much as possible should be.** Use as much of the CPU as possible while taking as little of the game thread as possible; the two halves are one goal, since the game's simulation stability is set by what runs on its own thread. The solver is already safe to split — order independence is an invariant, not an accident — and `MyAPIGateway.Parallel` provides `For`, `ForEach`, `Do` and `Start`. The natural shape is *solve in parallel, apply on the game thread* via `InvokeOnGameThread`, because reads must not race the game mutating a grid. **Measure before adopting, and note the two figures point opposite ways**: 8,000 blocks solve in 0.128 ms a step, possibly under the cost of a hand-off, while a 242-grid fleet spent 25.9 % of real time in the solver. Per-grid parallelism across many grids is a different change from splitting one huge grid, and the fleet figure argues for it first. How many threads, and whether the mod may saturate a machine it shares with other mods, is undecided. | [document-of-intent.md](document-of-intent.md#use-the-machine-and-stay-off-the-game-thread) |
| D1 | A solver step is atomic and 118 ms at a million blocks, down from 623 ms. A step must touch every node, so only activity tracking, chunking and multirate make it smaller; all designed, none built. Whether what is left is near the memory-bandwidth floor is now an open question rather than an answer — the last time that was claimed, two passes were reaching through the node objects instead. | [scale-design.md](scale-design.md), [load-and-hitching.md](load-and-hitching.md#what-is-still-open) |
| D2 | The room map floods the bounding volume — 14× the block count on a hull — and takes 7,207 ticks (twenty minutes) to converge at a million blocks. Wants the host gas system, with a coarse flood as fallback. | [scale-design.md](scale-design.md) §4 |
| D15 | **A frame spikes to 68 ms every thirty seconds and nothing says why.** Five frames of the 2026-08-20 fleet dump, evenly spaced, each reporting 2.0-2.8 ms of solver and zero topology, rooms and exposure against a 67-72 ms total. Nothing in the mod runs on that period — the mass sweep is 8 steps and the hottest-node scan 4. The environment sample and the after-step observation are now timed and the cost table carries an `unattributed` row, so the next dump attributes it or narrows it to pacing. | [telemetry.md](telemetry.md#what-the-stages-leave-over) |
| D4 | The first step of a grid's life spikes on first touch of every flat array: 28.3 ms above a 13.2 ms tick at half a million blocks, and 53 ms above a 26 ms tick at a million. A warm-up rather than a stutter, and still the largest number in the distribution at the top rung. | [known-issues.md](known-issues.md) |
| D3 | World load is 11 s at a million blocks in one call. Acceptable behind a loading screen; a blueprint pasted mid-session takes the same path. | [load-and-hitching.md](load-and-hitching.md#what-is-still-open) |
| D14 | **Spreading a step costs a large grid 5-13 % in cache.** Between two of its frames the other grids in the world evict its arrays, so every resume is a cold start. Staggering whole steps across frames instead would keep each grid's data hot and cost one dispatch, at the price of a lump per grid — the trade the current design was chosen to avoid, and worth re-deciding now the lump is measurable. | [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md#below-eight-hundred-blocks) |
| D13 | **A grid is visited every frame whether or not the frame does solver work.** `bench smallgrids` now measures that visit on its own — 5.9 ns a grid a frame, flat with grid size, 24 % of what a one-block grid costs and 70 % of its pacing overhead. No change to a step can reach it; only visiting fewer grids per frame can. | [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md#below-eight-hundred-blocks) |
| D5 | Lumping (merge a stiff node into its dominant neighbour at topology time) — exact where the substep cap is approximate, and removes links as well as stiffness. Worth a few per cent on SE1 fleets; the design work is unlumping incrementally on change. | [stiffness.md](stiffness.md#2-lumping--the-same-idea-done-properly) |
| D6 | Multirate stepping, and implicit integration as the endgame. Both currently lose to the per-block cap on an SE1 fleet; both are the answer when the stiff set is not a thin tail, i.e. SE2. | [stiffness.md](stiffness.md#3-multirate-stepping--when-the-tail-is-not-thin) |
| D18 | **The relaxation row is all ones for ninety-five per cent of nodes and is written for all of them.** Skipping the divide for the unbound ones was tried, is exact, and measured as nothing against a 4-6 % run-to-run spread — so the row's ~1 ns a node is the two loads and the store, not the arithmetic. A future attempt should aim at not writing the row: the conduction loop reads it per link end regardless, so this needs a sparse representation or a per-grid flag rather than a cheaper sum. Only live when a grid is refused the substeps it asked for. | [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md#the-relaxation-rows-cost-is-the-row-not-the-divide--tried-and-dropped) |

| H7 | **Two thirds of the corpus directory is files no walk can read, and a third of the files a walk does read hold no ship it will use.** Measured 2026-08-22: the corpus is 72.4 GB in 33,770 files, of which `Blueprints.Files` sees only the 10,016 `.sbc` — 50.3 GB. Dropping them from disk would shorten every sweep and bake today's two filters into the population — and `F14` records that the 25-block floor has never been varied to see whether it moves a population figure, so the filter is not yet a thing to make permanent.
